# StreamFlix — Business Analysis and Findings

## 1. Introduction

The purpose of this project was to analyse StreamFlix's subscription revenue, customer engagement, viewing behaviour and customer churn using SQL.

The dataset consists of three tables containing 12 registered users, 10 shows and 25 viewing records.

Throughout this project, I used SQL to answer business questions, identify patterns, handle data quality issues and develop recommendations that could help StreamFlix improve its business performance.

## 2. Revenue Analysis

Based on Q3, StreamFlix generates R1,242 in monthly subscription revenue from eight active subscribers.

In Q5, I found that each subscription plan had four registered users. However, this did not necessarily mean that each plan contributed equally to the company's revenue.

Following a discussion with my mentor, I created an additional query (Q19) to calculate the monthly revenue generated by each subscription plan.

| Subscription Plan | Active Users | Monthly Price | Monthly Revenue | Revenue Contribution |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Premium | 4 | R199 | R796 | 64.1% |
| Basic | 3 | R99 | R297 | 23.9% |
| Standard | 1 | R149 | R149 | 12.0% |
| Total | 8 | | R1,242 | 100% |

The calculations are:

- Premium: 4 × R199 = R796
- Basic: 3 × R99 = R297
- Standard: 1 × R149 = R149

Premium is the strongest revenue-generating subscription plan, contributing approximately 64.1% of the company's monthly subscription revenue.

This shows that StreamFlix should prioritise retaining its Premium customers while investigating ways to improve the performance of its Standard subscription plan.

## 3. Customer Churn Analysis

In Q16, I calculated the percentage of cancelled subscribers for each subscription plan.

| Subscription Plan | Total Users | Cancelled Users | Churn Rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Basic | 4 | 1 | 25% |
| Standard | 4 | 3 | 75% |
| Premium | 4 | 0 | 0% |

Standard has the highest churn rate at 75%, making it the subscription plan that requires the most attention.

Overall, four out of the 12 registered subscribers have cancelled, representing approximately 33.3% of the registered customer base.

In Q14, I identified three users who had no recorded viewing activity:

- Kagiso Molefe
- Tshepo Maseko
- Lebo Sithole

All three had cancelled their subscriptions.

To investigate this further, I created Q20 to compare the viewing activity of active and cancelled subscribers.

The results showed that 75% of cancelled subscribers had no recorded viewing activity, while all eight active subscribers had watched content.

### Correlation and Causation

During a discussion with my mentor, I asked whether we could conclude that customers cancelled because they were not watching anything.

I learned that although the results suggest a relationship between low engagement and customer churn, correlation does not necessarily mean causation.

Customers may have cancelled for other reasons, such as:

- Difficulty using the platform.
- Lack of interesting or relevant content.
- Subscription affordability.
- Technical difficulties.
- Billing or payment problems.
- Forgetting about their subscriptions.

To understand the actual reasons for cancellation, StreamFlix would need additional data, including cancellation surveys, login activity, customer support records, search history, customer acquisition channels and billing information.

### Potential Revenue Impact

In Q21, I calculated the monthly subscription value associated with cancelled accounts.

Standard accounted for R447, while Basic accounted for R99.

Together, these cancelled accounts represent R546 in potential monthly subscription revenue if all four customers returned at their listed prices.

This does not represent confirmed historical financial losses, but it highlights the potential financial benefit of improving customer retention.

## 5. Data Quality Analysis

During Q17, I identified inconsistent capitalisation in the country column.

South Africa appeared as both "South Africa" and "south africa".

Without correcting this inconsistency, SQL would treat them as separate groups when calculating the number of subscribers per country.

I used the LOWER() function to standardise the country names during analysis.

The corrected results were:

| Country | Total Users |
|---|---:|
| South Africa | 5 |
| Nigeria | 4 |
| Kenya | 3 |

South Africa has the largest registered customer base in the dataset.

Incorrect country information could affect regional marketing decisions, customer segmentation and business reporting.

I would recommend that the data team introduce standardised country names, dropdown selection fields and data validation rules during customer registration.

I also identified missing ages and a missing show rating during the analysis.

Instead of replacing missing values with incorrect information, I used IS NULL and appropriate CASE statements to handle them separately.

## 6. Business Recommendation

My main recommendation is for StreamFlix to introduce and test an early customer engagement and retention programme, particularly for Standard subscribers.

Standard has a churn rate of 75%, and three of the four cancelled subscribers had no recorded viewing activity.

StreamFlix could introduce personalised content recommendations, welcome messages and reminders for subscribers who have not started watching content.

Popular shows such as Township Tales could be recommended to encourage initial engagement.

The company should also collect cancellation feedback to understand whether customers are leaving because of content availability, pricing, technical difficulties or other reasons.

In Q22, I found that no currently active subscribers had missing viewing records. However, this query could be used regularly to identify active subscribers who have not yet watched anything.

StreamFlix should measure whether the proposed retention programme improves viewing activity and reduces cancellations before implementing it on a larger scale.

## 7. Limitations of the Analysis

One major limitation is the small size of the dataset. It contains only 12 users, 10 shows and 25 viewing records.

The results may therefore not accurately represent the behaviour of a larger streaming platform.

The dataset also does not contain customer feedback, detailed login activity, payment failure records or information about why customers cancelled.

Although the results suggest an association between low engagement and customer churn, they do not establish a causal relationship.

The calculated churn percentages represent the proportion of registered users who have cancelled in the available dataset, rather than a monthly churn rate measured over a defined period.

More comprehensive data would be required before making major business decisions.

## 8. Conclusion

This project helped me understand how SQL can be used to transform raw data into meaningful business insights.

The analysis identified Premium as the strongest revenue-generating and most-engaged subscription plan, Standard as the plan with the highest churn rate, and Drama as the genre with the highest total viewing time.

Through additional analysis and discussions with my mentor, I also learned the importance of asking further questions, validating conclusions and distinguishing correlation from causation.

By improving customer engagement, investigating cancellation reasons and maintaining accurate data, StreamFlix could make more informed decisions and improve customer retention and business performance.
